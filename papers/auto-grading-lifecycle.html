Paper №1 — The Claim / 論點

The auto-grading lifecycle. A corpus of interlinked documents that promotes and demotes its own entries by a PageRank-lineage score blended with trust seeds, editor curation, and public handpicks.

What the research found (2026-11-22): the scoring mechanics are copied from the giants — Brin & Page's PageRank, TrustRank, Eigenfactor, Penguin-era edge policy. But no turnkey score → promote/demote lifecycle machine exists anywhere — the lifecycle itself is Dawn's original contribution, and that is exactly what this paper claims. No more, no less.

How Google Ranks the Internet — Copyable Mechanics for Corpus Auto-Grading

Summary

Google did not rank the web by counting links. It solved a recursive eigenvector problem: a page is important if important pages link to it, and each page divides its importance equally among the pages it links to. That is PageRank (Brin & Page, 1998). Everything since — TrustRank, Penguin, nofollow, SpamBrain, E-E-A-T, freshness, RankBrain — is either (a) a change to which edges count and how much, (b) a change to where the random walk restarts (the teleport vector), or (c) a non-link quality/relevance signal blended on top. That decomposition is exactly how to build the corpus grader:

final_score(file) = blend( PageRank(graph) , TrustRank(seed-biased PR) , AntiTrust(spam) ,
                           HITS hub/authority , content-quality , freshness-decay , usage )
                  with edges pre-filtered / weighted / zeroed by anti-gaming rules

Copy PageRank verbatim first (formula, d=0.85, power iteration, dangling-node fix — §1, §6). Then copy the countermeasures with equal fidelity, because raw PageRank on a corpus whose authors can see the scoring rule will be gamed exactly as the web was: link farms → citation rings, self-links → self-citation loops, anchor bombs → invocation-label stuffing (§4, §8). No turnkey "auto-grade a wiki and promote/demote files" product was found; the closest real systems are Wikipedia/DBpedia PageRank, Eigenfactor for journals, and Obsidian-vault PageRank plugins — all scoring-only, none doing promotion/demotion lifecycle (§5).

Confidence: high on 1998–2004 mechanics (primary papers, read live); high on the existence/dates/effect of Penguin, nofollow, QDF, RankBrain, E-E-A-T (Google's own ranking-systems guide, read live); low on present-day Google weights — Google states only that PageRank "has evolved a lot" and remains core, and publishes no current formula. Anything below labelled patent only or inference for our corpus is not claimed as Google practice.

Findings

1. Original PageRank — in implementable detail

Source (verified live, 2026-11-22): Brin & Page, The Anatomy of a Large-Scale Hypertextual Web Search Engine, WWW7 1998 — https://site.uottawa.ca/~nelkadri/CSI5389/Papers/63-Google.pdf — §2.1–2.2. Companion: Page, Brin, Motwani & Winograd, The PageRank Citation Ranking (Stanford tech report, 1999).

1.1 The graph model

  • Nodes = documents. Directed edge u → v = u links to / cites v. Build the full citation graph first (Brin & Page built maps of 518 million hyperlinks over ~24M pages in the prototype).
  • Academic-citation intuition, made recursive: raw backlink count approximates importance, but "PageRank extends this idea by not counting links from all pages equally, and by normalizing by the number of links on a page."
  • Two ways a page gets high rank (paper, §2.1.2): many pages point to it, or a few high-PageRank pages point to it — e.g. one citation from the Yahoo! homepage outweighs many obscure citations. Both fall out of the same recursion; there is no separate rule.

1.2 The formula

Original form (paper §2.1.1), for page A with in-linkers T₁…Tₙ, C(T) = out-degree of T:

PR(A) = (1 − d)  +  d · Σᵢ  PR(Tᵢ) / C(Tᵢ)

Normalised form used by every modern implementation (identical ranking; scores are a probability distribution, Σ PR = 1):

PR(v) = (1−d)/N  +  d · Σ_{u→v} PR(u) / outdeg(u)          N = number of nodes
  • Rank flow: each node keeps nothing; it passes d · PR(u) onward, split equally across its out-edges. A page that links to 100 targets endorses each 1/100th as strongly as a page that links to 1 target endorses that target. Out-degree dilution is a built-in anti-spam property — hoarding out-links devalues each one.
  • Self-consistency: PR is defined in terms of PR. It is "the principal eigenvector of the normalized link matrix of the web" — i.e. the stationary distribution of the Markov chain below.

1.3 The random surfer and the Google matrix

Model: a surfer starts on a random page, repeatedly clicks a uniformly random out-link, but "eventually gets bored and starts on another random page." PR(v) = long-run fraction of time spent on v.

At each step:  with prob. d     follow a random out-link
               with prob. 1−d   teleport to a page drawn from distribution p (uniform by default)

Google matrix:  G = d·S + (1−d)·1·pᵀ
  S = column/row-normalised adjacency, dangling columns/rows patched (see 1.5)
  p = teleport / personalisation vector, Σ p = 1, default p(v)=1/N
Update:         r_{k+1} = Gᵀ r_k      (power method)

1.4 The damping factor — why ~0.85

  • Paper: "We usually set d to 0.85"; d is "the probability at each page the random surfer will get bored and request another random page."
  • Rationale reported in the secondary literature (index): d=0.85 was prompted by the observation that a user follows on the order of six hyperlinks before starting a new search — leakage 1−d ≈ 1/6. Expected run length before teleport is 1/(1−d) ≈ 6.7 clicks. Source: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1012.4870
  • It is empirical, not derived. Do not treat 0.85 as sacred for a document corpus: the same source notes Chen et al. found citation chains in scientific papers average ~2 links, making d = 0.5 more appropriate for citation networks. A markdown corpus is closer to a citation network than to aimless web surfing — start at 0.85 for comparability, but A/B 0.5–0.85 (§6).
  • d does three jobs at once:
  • Behavioural — models restarting.
  • Mathematical — makes G strictly positive ⇒ irreducible + aperiodic ⇒ by Perron–Frobenius a unique stationary distribution exists and power iteration converges to it from any start. All non-principal eigenvalues satisfy |λ| ≤ d.
  • Anti-gaming — caps how much rank any closed clique can trap (see rank sinks, §1.5) and bounds the payoff of a link farm: farm-internal circulation leaks (1−d) every step.
  • Trade-off: d→1 = purer link signal, slower convergence, more sink/spam sensitivity; d→0 = scores flatten toward the teleport vector (uniform = useless; seed-biased = TrustRank, §2.2).

1.5 Dangling nodes and rank sinks — the two fixes that make it well-defined

Sources (index): https://www.aicassindra.com/blogs/algorithms/pagerank_algorithm.html ; NetworkX implementation docs https://networkx.org/documentation/networkx-2.2/_modules/networkx/algorithms/link_analysis/pagerank_alg.html

  • Dangling node = no out-links (dead end). Naively its row is all zeros, so its rank leaks out of the system each iteration and Σ PR < 1. Standard fix: treat a dangling node as linking to every node according to the teleport vector p. Complete update:
r_{k+1}(v) = (1−d)·p(v) + d·[ Σ_{u→v, outdeg(u)>0} r_k(u)/outdeg(u)  +  D_k · p(v) ]
             where D_k = Σ_{u: outdeg(u)=0} r_k(u)     (total dangling mass this iteration)
  • Rank sink / spider trap = a closed set of nodes linking only among themselves. Under pure link-following (d=1) it absorbs all rank and never releases it; disconnected components likewise give non-unique answers. Teleportation is the fix: every step has (1−d) probability of escaping to anywhere, so a sink can hold at most its teleport share plus what genuinely flows in.
  • Orphans (in-degree 0) are the mirror problem: they receive only (1−d)·p(v) (+ dangling share) — the floor score. That is correct behaviour, not a bug: an uncited file should sit at the floor until something cites it (see §4 for the operational queue this creates).

1.6 Computation — power method

init:    r₀(v) = 1/N for all v        (any positive start works; uniform is standard)
repeat:  r_{k+1} = (1−d)·p + d·( link-flow term + dangling term )   [sparse matrix–vector multiply]
stop:    when ‖r_{k+1} − r_k‖₁ < tol      (NetworkX defaults: tol=1e-6 per node, max_iter=100)
  • Error shrinks by a factor ≈ d per iteration (rate governed by the second eigenvalue, ≤ d), so ~50–100 iterations reach 1e-6 at d=0.85 — independent of corpus size in iteration count.
  • Cost per iteration is O(edges), not O(N²): the graph is sparse (web average ≈10 out-links/page). Store adjacency as CSR/CSC; never materialise G.
  • Sanity checks every implementation must pass: scores ≥ 0; Σ r = 1 ± 1e-9; a node cited only by the top node outranks a node cited by many floor nodes.
  • Brin & Page's scale claim: PageRank for 26M pages computed "in a few hours on a medium size workstation" (1998 hardware). A markdown corpus (10³–10⁶ files) is trivial: full recompute nightly, or on every merge.

2. Companion / derivative algorithms

2.1 HITS — hubs and authorities (Kleinberg, 1998/1999)

Source (index): https://www.aicassindra.com/blogs/algorithms/hits_algorithm_hubs_authorities.html

Two mutually-reinforcing scores per node, not one:

auth(v) = Σ_{u→v} hub(u)        # a good authority is pointed to by good hubs
hub(u)  = Σ_{u→v} auth(v)       # a good hub points to good authorities
matrix: a = Mᵀh ,  h = M a   ⇒   a = principal eigenvector of MᵀM (co-citation)
                                 h = principal eigenvector of MMᵀ (bibliographic coupling)
iterate: start all =1, alternate updates, L2-normalise after each step (Σa²=Σh²=1) or values explode
  • Key difference from PageRank: HITS is query-dependent. Build a root set from a text search, expand by one link hop to a base set, run HITS only there. Same page can be an authority for one topic and nothing for another. PageRank is one global, query-independent vector.
  • Copyable value for a corpus: hub score ≈ index / map-of-content / survey file quality; authority score ≈ canonical reference quality. A file can be promoted as a hub (route readers through it) independently of being an authority. Also drop intrinsic links (same-site navigation) before running HITS, per Kleinberg — in corpus terms: templated/generated cross-references and nav footers are not endorsements.
  • Known flaw — Tightly-Knit Community (TKC) effect / topic drift: the densest cluster in the base set wins even if off-topic, because mutual reinforcement amplifies density, not relevance. Mitigations: SALSA (below), edge weighting, root-set purity.

2.2 TrustRank — seed-based trust propagation (Gyöngyi, Garcia-Molina & Pedersen, VLDB 2004)

Sources (index): primary paper https://www.cs.toronto.edu/vldb04/protected/eProceedings/contents/pdf/RS15P3.PDF ; worked summary https://algorithm.tamnd.com/trustrank/

TrustRank is literally Personalized PageRank with a human-curated teleport vector:

1. Seed selection: run Inverse PageRank (PageRank on the transposed graph) — pages that can
   reach many pages in few steps make the most informative seeds.
2. A human oracle labels a small seed set good/bad. Paper: effective with < 200 seed sites
   (evaluated on 31M AltaVista sites).
3. d_seed(v) = 1/|good seeds| if v is a good seed, else 0.
4. t = (1−α)·d_seed + α·T·t        (same power iteration; α ≈ 0.85)
   Variant in the paper: trust reaching a dangling node is dropped, not redistributed,
   so Σ t ≤ 1 — distance from seeds is itself penalised.
  • Load-bearing assumption — approximate isolation of the good set: "good pages seldom link to spam pages" (spam freely links to good pages — the asymmetry is the signal). Trust therefore attenuates with distance from seeds: 1 hop from a seed ≈ trustworthy; 5 hops ≈ unknown.
  • Use: not as a rank, but as a demotion counterweight — final = PageRank discounted where TrustRank is low, or flag high PageRank ∧ low TrustRank for human review. That pair is the link-spam signature.
  • Note the correction to SEO folklore: TrustRank is a Stanford/Yahoo paper, not a confirmed Google algorithm. Its mechanism (seed-biased teleport) is however exactly the personalisation Brin & Page themselves proposed: adding the damping/teleport mass "to a single page, or a group of pages… can make it nearly impossible to deliberately mislead the system" (Anatomy §2.1.2).

Anti-TrustRank (Krishnan & Raj, AIRWeb 2006, index: https://airweb.cse.lehigh.edu/2006/krishnan.pdf): mirror image — seed with known-bad pages, propagate distrust backwards along reversed edges (spam is pointed to by spam). Reported higher precision than TrustRank at finding high-PageRank spam. Copy both: trust flows forward from gold files; distrust flows backward from quarantined files.

3. How Google's scoring evolved beyond raw PageRank — and what translates

Source for current-system inventory (verified live): Google, A Guide to Google Search Ranking Systems — https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ranking-systems-guide — "PageRank, one of our core ranking systems used when Google first launched… How PageRank works has evolved a lot since then, and it continues to be part of our core ranking systems." Penguin and Panda are listed as retired into core (Penguin integrated 2016; Panda 2015; Helpful Content Mar 2024).

3.1 Anchor text — the link's label is indexed against the target

Anatomy §2.2 (verified live): most engines associate link text with the page containing it; Google also associates it with the page it points to, because "anchors often provide more accurate descriptions of web pages than the pages themselves" and anchors let non-text documents (images, programs, databases) be returned at all.

  • Translates directly: the citing sentence / invocation context in file A is metadata about file B. Index it against B: it supplies B's topic labels, synonyms and "what other files think B is for" — a signal B's author cannot unilaterally forge (well — see Google bombs, §4).
  • Failure mode it created: anchor-text over-optimisation and Google bombs (§4). Penguin specifically targets unnatural anchor concentration (e.g. a profile where most anchors are the identical keyword phrase).

3.2 Link spam, link farms, Penguin, nofollow, disavow, SpamBrain

Timeline (index sources in notes/sources.md):

Google's current spam-policy definition (index): link spam = "creating links to or from a site primarily for the purpose of manipulating search rankings" — paid links passing credit, excessive exchanges, automated link creation, keyword-rich anchors in distributed guest posts/press releases.

  • Translates: all of it, renamed — see mapping table (§7). The deepest lesson is the 2016 flip: ignore bad edges, don't punish their targets. In a corpus, demoting a file because other files cite it suspiciously lets any author sabotage a rival file. Zero the edges; demote only on evidence about the file itself or its outbound behaviour.
  • Does not translate: disavow as a user-facing tool (in a closed corpus you control the graph — just maintain an internal edge-blocklist); manual actions / reconsideration requests (replace with an appeals/audit log).

3.3 Quality signals & E-E-A-T

Sources (index): Google Search Quality Rater Guidelines overview https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/hsw-sqrg.pdf ; SEJ summary.

  • E-E-A-T = Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness (Experience added Dec 2022; Trust is central — "untrustworthy pages have low E-E-A-T no matter how Experienced, Expert, or Authoritative they may seem").
  • Critical correction: E-E-A-T is not a ranking factor and there is no E-E-A-T score. It is the rubric given to human quality raters; rater judgements are used to evaluate/train Google's automated systems, not to rank individual pages. Google's helpful-content docs say its systems use "a mix of factors that can identify content with good E-E-A-T."
  • Translates as a design pattern, not a formula: define a written quality rubric for files (first-hand evidence vs rehash; correctness; sourcing; maintained ownership), have humans grade a sample, and use those grades (a) as TrustRank seeds and (b) as training/validation data for any automated quality classifier. Do not invent a pseudo-precise "E-E-A-T score" field.
  • Adjacent Google systems that do translate as classifiers: Panda (2011, thin/duplicate/low-value content demotion) → near-duplicate & stub detection in the corpus; Helpful Content (2022→core 2024, content made "primarily for search traffic" vs for people) → content made primarily to harvest citations is the corpus analogue and should be a demotion feature.

3.4 Freshness — Query Deserves Freshness (QDF)

Sources: Google ranking-systems guide (verified live) — "various 'query deserves freshness' systems designed to show fresher content for queries where it would be expected"; QDF explainer (index) https://ahrefs.com/seo/glossary/query-deserves-freshness-qdf

  • Freshness is query-conditional, not universal: a spike in query volume + a spike in new documents on the topic up-weights recency for that query; evergreen queries barely move. Document-side signals: inception (first-crawl) date, magnitude of content change (cosmetic date-bumps are detected and ignored), rate of new in-links, anchor freshness. Google's 2011 freshness update famously affected ~35% of searches.
  • Translates, with a twist: a corpus grader should not apply a global recency bonus (that rewards churn). Apply (a) staleness decay per topic volatility class — fast-moving topics decay, foundational topics don't (this is where the mission's "damping → staleness decay" mapping actually belongs: it is QDF, not PageRank's d, that maps to decay); and (b) require substantive change (diff size threshold) to reset the freshness clock — copy Google's anti-date-bump rule verbatim.
  • Does not translate: query-volume spikes (no external query stream) — unless corpus search logs exist, in which case a topic suddenly being invoked/searched a lot is a legitimate QDF analogue.

3.5 Neural ranking — RankBrain, BERT, neural matching

Sources: Google guide (verified live): RankBrain "helps us understand how words are related to concepts… return relevant content even if it doesn't contain all the exact words"; neural matching = concept representations of queries and pages; BERT = word-combination meaning/intent. Contemporary reporting (index, Search Engine Land FAQ http://searchengineland.com/faq-all-about-the-new-google-rankbrain-algorithm-234440): RankBrain launched 2015, announced as Google's third-most-important signal, embedding language into vectors to handle the ~15% of daily queries never seen before.

  • What it is conceptually: learned semantic relevance (query ↔ document meaning) replacing/augmenting exact term matching. It is a relevance system, not an authority system — it does not replace PageRank, it sits beside it in the blend.
  • Translates only if the corpus is searched/queried: embedding similarity between a request and files, and embedding-based near-duplicate / topic-drift detection, are useful. Does not translate to grading: a file's rank/promotion should not rise because an embedding model finds it semantically central — that is gameable by keyword-dense restatement and conflates topicality with trustworthiness. Keep neural scores in the retrieval lane, out of the authority lane, exactly as Google keeps RankBrain (relevance) distinct from PageRank (link authority).

5. Non-Google implementations of PageRank-style corpus grading

Honest headline: no system was found that does the full lifecycle the mission wants (graph score → automatic promotion/demotion of knowledge files). What exists is scoring/ranking in four places:

  1. Wikipedia / DBpedia PageRank (Thalhammer & Rettinger, ESWC 2016 — https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-319-47602-5_41). PageRank computed over the Wikipedia article graph and published in DBpedia as entity-importance scores (used for knowledge-panel ranking). Their key experiment for us: weighting links by position/context in the article changes rankings materially, and template-generated links are a distortion (the Linnaeus/Taxobox case, §4 #2). Also 2DRank/CheiRank on all of English Wikipedia (6.85M articles, α=0.85 — https://arxiv.org/pdf/1006.4270).
  2. Eigenfactor / Article Influence (journals, in Clarivate's Journal Citation Reports) — PageRank grading of a citation corpus in production since 2007-ish, with the anti-gaming modifications in §2.3. The strongest proof that "PageRank + zeroed self-citations + time window" survives contact with motivated gamers (academics).
  3. UKB / Personalized PageRank on Wikipedia for word-sense disambiguation & semantic similarity (Agirre & Soroa) — PPR over the Wikipedia graph as a knowledge-base primitive, not a grader.
  4. Obsidian-vault tools (2025–26, small/indie): Clew (https://www.obsidianstats.com/plugins/clew) runs PageRank + betweenness + Louvain community detection over a vault to surface hub/bridge notes; obsidian-brain's rank_notes (https://glama.ai/mcp/servers/sweir1/obsidian-brain/tools/rank_notes) ranks notes by PageRank "influence" with two telling guards already built in — exclude notes with <2 in-links from influence ranking, and exclude unresolved stub targets (stubs "dominate eigenvector-style centrality even when they have no real content"); obsidian-llm-wiki uses Personalized PageRank over the [[wikilink]] graph for retrieval expansion. All are visualisation/retrieval, none promote/demote files — the lifecycle layer is the novel part of the mission's system and has no direct precedent to copy.

GRAPH BUILD
  nodes      = every file in inventory (including orphans — inventory, not crawl, defines N)
  edge u→v   = u cites/invokes v, deduplicated (multiple citations u→v count ONCE per edge class)
  drop       = self-edges (u→u); edges from generated/templated blocks (nav, auto-index, footers)
  classes    = editorial (weight 1.0) | mention/contextual (0.3–0.5, tune) | generated (0)
  weight     = w(u→v) by class × Reasonable-Surfer context factor (body ≫ list ≫ footer)

PAGERANK (global authority)
  d = 0.85 (baseline; test 0.5–0.7 — citation networks favour lower d)
  p = uniform 1/N
  r ← 1/N;  repeat r ← (1−d)p + d·( Σ_{u→v} r(u)·w(u→v)/Σ_v w(u→·)  +  dangling_mass·p )
  until ‖Δr‖₁ < 1e-6  (expect 50–100 iters; O(iters × edges); assert Σr=1)

TRUSTRANK (seed authority) — same solver, p = uniform over human-approved "gold" files only
  seeds chosen by Inverse PageRank (PR on transposed graph) + human labelling; ~0.1–1% of corpus
ANTI-TRUSTRANK — same solver on reversed graph, p = uniform over quarantined files

HITS (per topic cluster, optional) — a = Mᵀh, h = M a, L2-normalise, ~20–50 iters; or SALSA

FINAL GRADE (example blend — weights are OURS to tune, not Google's)
  grade = z(PageRank) + z(TrustRank) − z(AntiTrust) + quality_classifier + freshness_factor(topic)
  freshness_factor: exp decay with per-topic half-life, reset ONLY by substantive diff (>~10–30% of body)
PROMOTION / DEMOTION
  promote: grade crosses upper threshold AND TrustRank > floor AND in-degree from ≥2 independent
           author-clusters AND no cartel flag   →  hysteresis: demote threshold < promote threshold
  demote:  grade crosses lower threshold, OR AntiTrust flag, OR quality-classifier fail
  NEVER demote on inbound-edge evidence alone (Penguin-4.0 doctrine)
AUDIT (every recompute): top sinks, new SCCs/cartel candidates, reciprocity outliers,
  anchor-concentration outliers, orphan list, dangling high-rank list, largest grade movers

Parameters summary: d=0.85 (test 0.5), tol=1e-6, max_iter=100 (NetworkX defaults, index: NetworkX docs above), HITS normalisation L2, Eigenfactor window 5 yrs / corpus analogue = only citations from files updated in the window count at full weight.

8. Countermeasures to build in from day one

  1. Zero self-edges and cap same-author/same-cluster edges before the first run (Eigenfactor diagonal rule). Retrofitting this after ranks are published is a political fight; building it in is a line of code.
  2. Deduplicate and class every edge by provenance (editorial / mention / generated-templated). Generated index & nav links at weight 0 — the Wikipedia Taxobox lesson.
  3. Seed-biased trust from day one: hand-approve a gold set (even 20–50 files), run TrustRank alongside PageRank, and require a TrustRank floor for promotion. Uniform-teleport PageRank alone gives cartels a fair fight; seed-biased teleport doesn't.
  4. Neutralise, don't punish (Penguin 4.0): suspicious inbound edges go to weight 0; a file is demoted only for its own content/outbound behaviour. This kills sabotage incentives at birth.
  5. Independent-endorsement requirement for promotion: ≥2 (better: ≥3) in-citations from disjoint author/directory clusters — the corpus equivalent of "links from independent domains."
  6. Cartel surveillance in the recompute job: SCC density, reciprocity ratio, and "external in-link share" per cluster; clusters whose citations are >~80% internal get their collective outflow capped pending review.
  7. Anchor/invocation-label corroboration: a file gains topic authority from invocation labels only if its own content matches the label (anti-Google-bomb).
  8. Substantive-change freshness: freshness clock resets only on diffs above a size threshold; date/frontmatter bumps alone do nothing (Google's anti-date-bump rule).
  9. Hysteresis + mover review: promote threshold > demote threshold; any file whose grade moves more than X percentiles in one recompute is queued for human glance before the change takes effect. Prevents oscillation and silent coups.
  10. Orphan & dead-end queues as first-class outputs, not errors: every recompute publishes in-degree-0 files (candidates for linking or deletion) and high-rank dangling files (curation failures).
  11. Keep the grade explainable: for every file, store its top contributing in-edges and its trust distance from seeds. Google could afford opacity; an internal grader that authors must trust cannot — and explainability is the cheapest anti-gaming audit there is.
  12. Don't publish the full formula/weights to corpus authors (Goodhart). Publish the principles and the rubric; keep exact blend weights and cartel thresholds internal, and re-tune them as gaming adapts — Google's 25-year pattern is an arms race, not a one-time fix.

Could not verify

  • Google's current PageRank formula/weight in the overall blend. Google confirms only that PageRank remains a core system and "has evolved a lot." Any source claiming a current exact formula or percentage weight is speculation; none is relied on here.
  • Whether Google ever used TrustRank or the Reasonable Surfer model in production. Both are papers/patents from Google-adjacent authors; neither is confirmed as deployed. They are recommended here on their mechanics, not on Google's authority.
  • A turnkey wiki/KB auto-grading product (score → automatic promotion/demotion lifecycle): none found despite targeted searches of Obsidian/Logseq/GitHub ecosystems (§5). The lifecycle layer appears to be novel build, not copyable prior art.
  • Exact TrustRank α and seed count for a small corpus: the paper's <200 seeds / 31M sites ratio doesn't scale down mechanically; seed fraction must be tuned empirically.
